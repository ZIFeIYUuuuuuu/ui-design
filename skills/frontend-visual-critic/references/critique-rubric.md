# Visual Critique Rubric

This rubric is a structured aid for comparing iterations of the same task. It is not an objective or universal aesthetic benchmark.

## Dimensions

Rate each from 1 to 5 and attach observable evidence.

| Dimension | Weight | Evaluate |
|---|---:|---|
| Visual Quality | 35 | Composition, hierarchy, typography, color, spacing, surfaces, imagery, page rhythm |
| Originality / Art Direction | 25 | Coherent concept and product-specific decisions, without novelty for novelty's sake |
| Interaction Quality | 25 | Discoverability, feedback, continuity, motion purpose, keyboard/touch behavior |
| Craft | 15 | Alignment, details, consistency, responsive polish, state completeness, asset quality |
| **Total** | **100** | |

Formula: sum of (rating / 5 × weight).

## Rating anchors

### 1 — Broken / very weak
- No coherent hierarchy or composition.
- Typography and spacing appear arbitrary.
- Important content or actions are hard to find.
- Interactions are absent, misleading, or visibly broken.
- Result substantially misses the user's stated direction.

### 2 — Weak
- Usable structure exists, but the page feels generic, flat, inconsistent, or unfinished.
- Repeated template patterns dominate.
- Visual emphasis is misplaced.
- Important states or responsive behavior are missing.
- Effects are superficial or poorly integrated.

### 3 — Competent
- Clear, usable, and reasonably consistent.
- Hierarchy and spacing mostly work.
- Direction is recognisable but familiar.
- Main interactions work.
- Some details, states, or responsive refinements remain.

### 4 — Strong
- Distinct and coherent direction suited to the product.
- Strong composition, typography, hierarchy, and material choices.
- Details reinforce rather than compete with the task.
- Interactions feel deliberate and integrated.
- Responsive and state behavior are carefully handled.

### 5 — Exceptional
- Highly coherent, memorable, and product-specific.
- Composition and typography show strong judgement.
- Every visual and interactive choice has a purpose.
- Excellent craft across states and viewports.
- No meaningful quality issue remains in the reviewed scope.

## Score discipline

- Compare the same task, content, data, and viewports across iterations.
- Do not compare unrelated products by total score.
- Do not reward unusual colors, 3D effects, or visual complexity alone.
- A conventional design can score highly when it is right for the product.
- If a hard gate fails, mark the score provisional and state the failure.
- If a dimension is UNKNOWN or not applicable, mark it unscored and omit or transparently normalize the total.
- The score ranks alternatives; it does not replace written critique or human approval.
